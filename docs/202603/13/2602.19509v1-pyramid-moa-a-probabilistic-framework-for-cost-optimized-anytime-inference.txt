Abstract 

Large Language Models (LLMs) face a persistent trade-off between inference cost and reasoning capability. While "Oracle" models (e.g., Llama-3-70B) achieve state-of-the-art accuracy, they are prohibitively expensive for high-volume deployment. Smaller models (e.g., 8B parameters) are cost-effective but struggle with complex tasks. In this work, we propose "Pyramid MoA" , a hierarchical Mixture-of-Agents architecture that uses a lightweight Router to dynamically escalate queries only when necessary. By leveraging semantic agreement and confidence calibration among an ensemble of small models, our Router identifies "hard" problems with high precision. On the GSM8K benchmark, our system achieves 93.0% accuracy , effectively matching the Oracle baseline (98.0%) while reducing compute costs by 61% . We demonstrate that the system introduces negligible latency overhead (+0.82s) and allows for a tunable trade-off between performance and budget. 

# 1 Introduction 

The rapid proliferation of Large Language Models (LLMs) has created a dichotomy in deployment strategies: utilize "Small Language Models" (SLMs) for low-latency, cost-effective inference, or "Oracle" models (e.g., 70B+ parameters) for high-stakes reasoning. Recent Mixture-of-Agents (MoA) approaches attempt to bridge this by layering models. While emerging works like Sparse MoA [5] and Residual MoA 

(RMoA) [ 6] have begun to explore adaptive termination, they often require architectural modifications or complex internal metrics. We reframe the MoA paradigm through the lens of Anytime Algorithms [ 1], proposing a probabilistic anytime approach to multi-agent routing. Unlike architectural variants that rely on complex internal metrics, we introduce Pyramid MoA , a framework governed by a distinct, model-agnostic Decision-Theoretic Router. This offers a transparent trade-off between solution quality and computational resource consumption. 

1. The Pyramid Architecture & Value of Computation: We treat model selection as a decision-theoretic problem. The "pyramid" structure refers to the hierarchical escalation of computational effort and query volume. The system begins with a wide base, where all queries are processed by a cost-effective layer of SLMs. A lightweight Router then estimates the Value of Computation (VoC) —specifically, the probability that escalating to a higher-capability tier will correct an error. The workload forms a pyramid as only a filtered subset of increasingly difficult queries ascends to the more computationally expensive models. 

2. Probabilistic Anytime Property: Classical anytime algorithms produce a valid solution immediately and improve it iteratively over time. Pyramid MoA exhibits a "probabilistic" anytime property: while we cannot guarantee monotonic improvement for every single instance due to LLM stochasticity, the expected 

solution quality strictly increases as queries are routed deeper into the network. 

3. Bridging the Gap: Unlike static MoA frameworks, our system halts computation early for 61% of queries (on GSM8K), achieving 95% of the Oracle’s performance profile while incurring only 39% of the cost. This validates the "Anytime" hypothesis: high-quality reasoning does not require high-cost compute for every query, but rather a mechanism to identify when high-cost compute is necessary. 1

# 2 Methodology 

2.1 System Components 

Our architecture consists of three components: 1. Layer 1 (The Crowd): An ensemble of cost-effective models (Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B, Qwen-2.5-7B). 2. The Router: A trained classifier predicting the probability of Layer 1 failure ( Pf ail ). 3. Layer 2 (The Oracle): A state-of-the-art model (Llama-3-70B) invoked only when Pf ail > t ,where t is a tunable threshold. 

Note on Nomenclature (Routing vs. Generation): Unlike generative MoA approaches that synthesize a new response from peer outputs [ 3], our framework employs a Routing-Based MoA . Here, the ‘mixture’ is utilized to calibrate the decision boundary for escalation rather than to generate the token sequence itself. This allows us to retain the efficiency benefits of small models while leveraging the collective signal of the ensemble to identify "hard" queries.

2.2 Decision-Theoretic Routing 

To formalize the routing decision, we define the Value of Computation (VoC) as the expected utility gain from escalating to the Oracle. Let Ucorrect be the utility of a correct answer (e.g., 1) and Cesc be the 2relative cost of invoking the Oracle. The expected utility of stopping at Layer 1 is: 

E[Ustop ] = (1 − Pf ail ) · Ucorrect (1) The expected utility of escalating to Layer 2 (assuming the Oracle is significantly more capable, i.e., 

P (Success |L2) ≈ 1) is: 

E[Uesc ] = 1 · Ucorrect − Cesc (2) Escalation is optimal when E[Uesc ] > E [Ustop ]. Substituting and simplifying yields the decision rule:

# 4 Related Work 

Mixture-of-Agents (MoA): Collaborative layers of LLMs can outperform individual state-of-the-art models [ 3]. However, standard MoA implementations execute a pre-determined number of layers for all inputs, ignoring the heterogeneous difficulty of user queries. 

Efficient MoA Variants: Recent research has focused on reducing computational overhead. Self-MoA 

[ 4] demonstrates that iteratively querying a single strong model can sometimes outperform diverse 5ensembles. Sparse MoA (SMoA) [5 ] and Residual MoA (RMoA) [ 6] introduce early stopping and residual connections to reduce compute. RMoA, in particular, achieves significant gains on benchmarks like MATH by utilizing adaptive termination. 

Differentiation: Unlike RMoA, which relies on architectural residuals, our Pyramid MoA employs a dis-tinct decision-theoretic approach. We utilize a separated, lightweight classifier (Random Forest/XGBoost) to estimate the "Value of Computation" based on semantic agreement features, allowing for a strictly modular "SLM-to-Oracle" handoff compatible with black-box model APIs. 

Anytime Algorithms & VoC: The concept of trading execution time for quality is grounded in the work of Hansen and Zilberstein [ 2]. We extend this to Generative AI by utilizing ensemble agreement to estimate failure probability. This aligns with "early exit" strategies in deep learning such as BranchyNet [8]. 

# 5 Discussion & Conclusion 

We presented Pyramid MoA, a framework that democratizes access to high-end reasoning capabilities. By filtering 61% of queries that can be solved by smaller models, we demonstrate that massive resource savings are possible without significant compromises in accuracy. Future work will explore extending this architecture to other domains such as code generation and legal reasoning.
